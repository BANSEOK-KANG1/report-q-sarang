## Abstract
Cordyceps militaris is a medicinal fungus valued for its production of bioactive nucleosides, particularly cordycepin and adenosine. In this study, the effects of substrate type and cultivation duration on nucleoside production were systematically investigated using sugarcane bagasse, brown rice, and coconut broth–based media under controlled laboratory conditions. Samples collected on Days 8, 16, and 30 were analyzed by HPLC to quantify cordycepin and adenosine levels. The results revealed distinct substrate-dependent production patterns, with cordycepin generally increasing over time, while adenosine either decreased or remained stable, leading to a progressive rise in the cordycepin-to-adenosine ratio. By integrating concentration profiles, metabolic ratios, and production rates, three characteristic cordycepin production modes were identified: Delayed Induction Mode (DIM), Continuous Production Mode (CPM), and Biphasic Conversion Mode (BCM). These modes reflect different metabolic strategies, including precursor accumulation followed by conversion, parallel biosynthetic pathways, and spatially coupled metabolite transfer and transformation between solid and liquid phases. Furthermore, a biosynthetic framework was proposed in which adenosine functions not only as a precursor but also as a key regulatory metabolite. Its dual role in supporting cordycepin biosynthesis via adenosine-3′-monophosphate and stabilizing cordycepin through pentostatin-mediated inhibition of adenosine deaminase provides a mechanistic basis for the observed production modes. Overall, this study highlights the critical roles of substrate composition and cultivation time in modulating nucleoside metabolism and offers new insights into optimizing fermentation strategies for regulating cordycepin production in Cordyceps militaris.
