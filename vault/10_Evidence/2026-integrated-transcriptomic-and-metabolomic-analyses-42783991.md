## Abstract
<i>Cordyceps militaris</i> is an edible entomopathogenic fungus with substantial nutritional and medicinal value. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying the vegetative mycelia-to-primordia (PR) transition and bioactive metabolite synthesis are still poorly defined. Deletion of the acyl-CoA dehydrogenase gene (<i>ACAD</i>) caused malformed, stunted PR-like structures together with excessive aerial mycelial growth, severely disrupting PR initiation and fruiting-body elongation in <i>C. militaris</i>. Transcriptomic, untargeted metabolomic and multi-omics analyses were performed on wild-type (WT) and <i>ACAD</i>-knockout strains at the mycelial and PR stages to explore gene expression reprogramming and bioactive nucleoside accumulation during the developmental transition and under <i>ACAD</i> deficiency. In total, 2014, 448, and 838 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified in WT PR vs. WT mycelia, Δ<i>ACAD</i> mycelia vs. WT mycelia, and Δ<i>ACAD</i> PR vs. WT PR, demonstrating extensive transcriptional reprogramming during the development switch and upon <i>ACAD</i> deletion. KEGG enrichment and WGCNA analyses showed that <i>ACAD</i> deficiency significantly suppresses multiple reproductive-development pathways, including cell cycle progression, purine biosynthesis, the TCA cycle, MAPK signaling, redox homeostasis, fatty acid and amino acid metabolism. Consistently, <i>ACAD</i> deletion markedly increased intracellular ROS level and substantially reduced cellular ATP levels in mycelia. Several crucial genes modulated by <i>ACAD</i> were identified, including GTP cyclohydrolase I, histone lysine N-methyltransferase ASHR1, adenine phosphoribosyl transferase 1, glutathione-S-transferase F3 and CoA-ligase CCL8. Multi-omics integration demonstrated tightly coordinated transcriptional and metabolic regulation dependent on <i>ACAD</i> function. <i>ACAD</i> deficiency significantly disturbed amino acid, lipid, and nucleotide metabolism and reduced the abundance of amino acid derivatives, lipid compounds and purine nucleoside precursors in mycelia. Particularly, adenosine analogs serve as key precursors of the core bioactive nucleoside cordycepin. Key DEGs, including peroxisomal (S)-2-hydroxyacid oxidase, isocitrate lyase 2 and L-ascorbate peroxidase 5, were linked to these metabolic shifts. Our findings demonstrate that <i>ACAD</i> serves as an essential regulator that interconnects carbon metabolism, redox homeostasis, lipid, amino acid and purine metabolism to promote PR formation and secondary metabolite synthesis in <i>C. militaris</i>.
