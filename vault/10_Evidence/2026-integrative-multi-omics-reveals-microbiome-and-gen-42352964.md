## Abstract
Chinese <i>Cordyceps</i> (<i>Ophiocordyceps sinensis</i>) and Xinjiang <i>Cordyceps</i> (<i>Paraisaria gracilis</i>) are related entomopathogenic fungi that occupy different elevations and habitats. Whether their holobiont architectures have diverged accordingly is unknown. In this hypothesis-generating study based on samples from single locations (Altai Mountains for Xinjiang <i>Cordyceps</i> and Nagqu, Tibet for Chinese <i>Cordyceps</i>), we compared the two species using amplicon sequencing, untargeted metabolomics, and comparative genomics. Chinese <i>Cordyceps</i> from the sampled site comprises a specialized parasitic fungus and host-adapted bacteria for nutrient acquisition. Xinjiang <i>Cordyceps</i> from the Altai site contains diverse saprotrophic fungi and a rhizosphere-like bacterial consortium enriched in oxidative defense and biofilm genes, a finding that may explain why its sclerotia remain intact for 3-5 years in this population. Metabolomic profiles distinguish the two species at these sites. Xinjiang <i>Cordyceps</i> shows upregulation of tyrosine and porphyrin pathways, and its bacterial community shows functional enrichment in the same pathways, suggesting cross-kingdom coordination. <i>P. gracilis</i> has lost many gene families, and the retained species-specific genes are linked to cell adhesion and acyltransferase activity. Xinjiang <i>Cordyceps</i> is not a simple substitute for Chinese <i>Cordyceps</i> but appears to represent a different ecological strategy shaped by genome streamlining and host-microbe coadaptation. Our findings generate testable hypotheses for future large-scale, multi-population investigations.
