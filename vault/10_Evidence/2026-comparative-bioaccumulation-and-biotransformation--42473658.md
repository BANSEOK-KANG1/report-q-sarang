## Abstract
Selenium (Se) is an essential trace element for humans. <i>Cordyceps militaris</i> is an edible mushroom with nutritional value. To develop a selenium biofortification strategy for <i>C</i>. <i>militaris</i>, the absorption and transformation of selenium from selenate, selenite, and selenomethionine in mycelia were investigated. The half maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC<sub>50</sub>) of selenate, selenite and selenomethionine were 1028 mg L<sup>-1</sup>, 68.2 mg L<sup>-1</sup>, and 1.5 mg L<sup>-1</sup>, respectively. The kinetic fitting results indicated that the absorption rate of selenomethionine is higher than that of selenite and selenate. The absorption of selenium was inhibited by carbonyl cyanide m-chlorophenylhydrazone (CCCP) and 2,4-dinitrophenol (2,4-DNP), while the addition of sulfur, phosphorus, and methionine reduced the uptake of selenate, selenite, and selenomethionine by 80.6%, 81.9%, and 14.2%, respectively. The mycelium can convert selenite into elemental selenium at 40 mg L<sup>-1</sup>. The major selenium species in selenium-enriched mycelium are selenomethionine, selenocysteine, and Se-methylselenocysteine. Our results indicate that selenium biofortification of <i>C</i>. <i>militaris</i> mycelium using selenite is the optimal strategy. The selenium-enriched <i>C</i>. <i>militaris</i> mycelium could serve as a novel selenium source in human diets.
